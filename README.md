# Groundwater Nitrate Atlas — Chalk vs Clay

https://nellchris.github.io/groundwater-nitrate-atlas/
This project investigates nitrate contamination in groundwater across part of southern England. Nitrate from agricultural fertilisers and livestock can seep through soil into underground water sources, posing a risk to drinking water. The study area spans Dorset, Wiltshire, Hampshire and parts of the surrounding counties — a region where permeable chalk aquifers sit alongside low-permeability clay formations, creating a natural contrast in how vulnerable groundwater is to surface pollution.

## Results

Wells on chalk have a median nitrate concentration of 37.9 milligrams per litre. One in five exceeds the drinking-water limit of 50 milligrams per litre (Water Supply (Water Quality) Regulations 2016). Wells on clay are almost all below the detection limit — the typical clay well returns no measurable nitrate at all.

Within the chalk, surrounding land use matters. Wells with high farmland coverage show a median of 42.5 compared to 27.0 for those with less farmland, and 29.3% of high-farmland chalk wells exceed the limit versus just 2.5% of low-farmland ones. Source Protection Zone status makes no measurable difference — chalk wells inside and outside these zones show nearly identical nitrate levels. This is expected: the zones are defined by groundwater travel time to an abstraction point, not by nitrate loading at the surface.

## Method

Groundwater nitrate measurements were collected from the Environment Agency's Water Quality Archive for 215 monitoring wells, covering samples from late 2021 to late 2026. Each well was reduced to a single median value from its most recent five years of data. Wells were classified by the rock type at their location using the British Geological Survey's Hydrogeology 625k map, grouping them into chalk (highly permeable) and clay/low-permeability categories. Two additional layers were overlaid: Source Protection Zones, which are regulatory boundaries drawn around drinking-water abstraction points, and farmland coverage derived from ESA WorldCover 2021 satellite imagery, combining cropland and grassland within a one-kilometre radius of each well.
